## P06 — Priyadarshni & Pandey (2025), UHI in India review
- **Why I read it:** Where Indian UHI research stands and where the gaps are
- **Main takeaway:** Indian UHI research is mostly satellite-based surface studies concentrated in big metros; smaller cities, equity and policy links are under-studied.
- **Key numbers:** 89 studies, 40+ cities; Delhi most studied (13%); urban 2–10 °C warmer than surroundings
- **Gap / limitation:** Few studies of non-megacities; little equity analysis; weak link to policy
- **How I'll use it:** Justify choosing Gurugram (not Delhi) and linking results to GMDA's plan; cite in Introduction and Gap statement

## P07 — Rasul et al. (2017), Review on remote sensing of urban heat and cool islands
- **Why I read it:** Why can a semi-arid city be cooler than its surroundings by day, and what does that mean for my study?
- **Main takeaway:** In arid/semi-arid regions, cities can be cooler than bare surroundings by day (urban cool island) and warmer at night, because bare land heats and cools fast while urban materials store heat (high thermal inertia). Such climates are under-studied.
- **Key numbers:** — (review)
- **Gap / limitation:** Few LST–land cover studies in arid/semi-arid cities; need higher-resolution data, field data, night-time studies, attention to surrounding land
- **How I'll use it:** Justifies separating bare land from built-up (Dynamic World); caution choosing rural reference areas; state that daytime Landsat misses night-time heat; cite in Gap statement

## P08 — Zheng et al. (2026), Systematic review of UHI impacts and mitigation
- **Why I read it:** What reduces urban heat, for whom, and how should policy target it?
- **Main takeaway:** Heat harms health mainly through air temperature and hits disadvantaged groups hardest; mitigation works but benefit depends on the neighbourhood, so policy must be spatially targeted and coordinated across sectors.
- **Key numbers:** Green infrastructure ~1–6 °C cooling; cool surfaces ~2–22 °C; disadvantaged groups ~3–4 °C greater exposure
- **Gap / limitation:** 33 studies, mostly N. America/Europe/Asia; surface vs air temperature not interchangeable; pandemic effects reflect emergency conditions; some figures combine unlike comparisons
- **How I'll use it:** Supports ranking priority areas and matching interventions to area type; keep LST ≠ air temperature/health in claims; cite in policy section

## P09 — Weng, Lu & Schubring (2004), Remote Sensing of Environment 89: 467–483
- **Why I read it:** Is NDVI the best vegetation measure for LST studies? Does scale matter?
- **Main takeaway:** LST relates slightly more strongly to vegetation fraction than to NDVI; the relationship changes with pixel size and was strongest around 120 m.
- **Key numbers:** Scales tested 30–960 m; strongest ≈ 120 m
- **Gap / limitation:** One temperate US city, one date; old sensor
- **How I'll use it:** (1) Justifies going beyond plain NDVI — Dynamic World % trees/grass per cell. (2) Supports robustness checks at 250 m, 500 m and 1 km.

## P10 — Brown et al. (2022), Dynamic World, Scientific Data 9: 251
- **Why I read it:** Is there a dataset that separates bare land from built-up, and trees from grass?
- **Main takeaway:** Free global 10 m land-cover dataset in Earth Engine with 9 classes; each pixel has a probability for every class.
- **Key numbers:** 10 m; 2015–present; Sentinel-2
- **Gap / limitation:** Classification errors possible — check locally; 10 m vs 30 m Landsat — match season, summarise per cell
- **How I'll use it:** Average class probabilities per cell → % trees, grass, built, bare, water. Solves bare vs built, trees vs grass, water masking. Cite in Data section.
